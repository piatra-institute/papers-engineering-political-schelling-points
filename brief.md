# Brief

Written before research began; revised once the model was solved (see audit.md).

## Question

When many accounts amplify a call to collective action, how much of the message's effect on participation comes from its value as evidence about the underlying situation, and how much from its value as a shared reference that lets people predict what others will do? Under what exact conditions does disclosing that the accounts share one source raise, leave unchanged or lower participation, and when does a temporary message leave a change that outlasts it?

## Claim

In a global game of regime change with private signals, a message whose perceived precision depends on believed source independence, and a separate reach parameter for whether receipt is common knowledge among receivers, the equilibrium participation cutoff decomposes exactly into an evidential channel (closed form, linear in perceived precision) and a coordination channel (an exact multiplier that rises with reach). Coordination value is parasitic on evidential value somewhere in the belief hierarchy: a message that every receiver believes uninformative has no effect however visible, while a privately disclosed common source leaves the disclosed receiver responding to the message through others' credulity. Public disclosure that also makes receipt common knowledge raises participation in a mobilizing message exactly when the gain in the coordination multiplier exceeds the deflation of perceived precision. With erosion of the status quo by past participation, a temporary message has persistent effects only above a closed-form feedback threshold and only if it pushes participation past the unstable state; disclosure after that crossing does not reverse it.

## Kind

Formal model with exact equilibrium computation (recorded run), plus a decisive-experiment design with model-derived effect sizes, a simulation-based power analysis and an identifiability result. No human-subject data; no empirical test on public data.

## Strongest serious alternative

The effect of coordinated amplification is mostly persuasion (changing private beliefs or valuations) or a majority illusion produced by network sampling, and the public-signal overweighting of Morris and Shin already explains any amplification of public messages; nothing about provenance or reach is needed. A second alternative: in a unique-equilibrium global game a message without information cannot coordinate, so "coordination value without evidential value" is impossible and the publicity paradox is a slogan.
