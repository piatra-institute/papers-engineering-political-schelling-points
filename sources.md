# Sources

Frozen bibliography for the paper. Identity of every entry was checked on 2026-11-18 against Crossref (DOI records), RePEc/IDEAS (Morris and Shin 1998), OpenAlex or OpenLibrary (books). "Inspected" says what text was actually read; page numbers refer to the version named. Where only a working-paper version was read, the paper's claims are limited to what the published abstract and the working paper agree on, and the source claim's note says so. Files were downloaded into a scratch directory and read as text (pdftotext) or, for scanned or font-shifted PDFs, as page images; sha256 digests are recorded below so a reviewer can confirm the same file.

## Global games and public information

- **carlsson1993**. Carlsson, H. and van Damme, E. (1993). Global games and equilibrium selection. *Econometrica* 61(5), 989-1018. DOI 10.2307/2951491 (Crossref). Inspected: abstract only (OpenAlex). Used for attribution of the global-games selection result; no source claim.
- **morris1998**. Morris, S. and Shin, H. S. (1998). Unique equilibrium in a model of self-fulfilling currency attacks. *American Economic Review* 88(3), 587-597. No DOI; identity from the RePEc/IDEAS record (aea:aecrev:v:88:y:1998:i:3:p:587-97). Not inspected; attribution only.
- **morris2002**. Morris, S. and Shin, H. S. (2002). Social value of public information. *AER* 92(5), 1521-1534. DOI checked. Inspected: full text of the working paper, Cowles Foundation Discussion Paper 1312, "The CNBC Effect: Welfare Effects of Public Information" (July 2001), Wayback copy of cowles.yale.edu, sha256 f69f81c7...; read from page images because the PDF's font is shifted. Supports: equilibrium action (2.12) gives the public signal more than its relative precision (WP p. 15); welfare falls with public precision when r > 1/2 and private information is precise enough (WP pp. 15-16, condition 3.5). Published article not read.
- **morris2003**. Morris, S. and Shin, H. S. (2003). Global games: theory and applications. In *Advances in Economics and Econometrics, Eighth World Congress*, Vol. 1, CUP, 56-114. Crossref lists DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511610240.004 but its record carries no year, so the entry omits the DOI to keep the identity check meaningful; the reviewer can confirm identity from the CUP page. Inspected: CFDP 1275R (August 2001), sha256 28a17c47..., read from page images (shifted font). Supports the "publicity" effect and that its multiplier is largest just before the multiplicity region (WP pp. 30-32). Caution recorded: the uniqueness condition in this working paper is stated for a linear investment game, not the regime-change game; the paper therefore cites Angeletos, Hellwig and Pavan (2007) for the regime-change condition.
- **hellwig2002**. Hellwig, C. (2002). Public information, private information, and the multiplicity of equilibria in coordination games. *JET* 107(2), 191-222. DOI checked. Inspected: LSE FMG Discussion Paper 361 (October 2000, same title), scanned, read from page images; sha256 76b59459.... Supports: multiplicity more likely when overall noise is low and public information relatively informative (WP abstract). Published article and abstract not obtained (publisher blocked); source claim at abstract depth of the working paper.
- **angeletos2006**. Angeletos, Hellwig and Pavan (2006). Signaling in a global game: coordination and policy traps. *JPE* 114(3), 452-484. DOI checked. Inspected: full text, typeset version (author-hosted), sha256 76e87b97.... Supports: the informational role of policy leads to multiple equilibria (abstract, pp. 452-454); the result does not rely on the policy being a public signal, a point the paper's wording respects.
- **angeletos2007**. Angeletos, Hellwig and Pavan (2007). Dynamic global games of regime change. *Econometrica* 75(3), 711-756. DOI checked. Inspected: full text (author-hosted), sha256 e4fe0cd9.... Supports: static uniqueness iff beta >= alpha^2/(2 pi) and limit threshold 1 - c (Proposition 1, p. 719); multiplicity under the same conditions in the dynamic game and phases of tranquillity and distress without changes in fundamentals (abstract, p. 711).

## Regime change, protest and manipulation

- **buenodemesquita2010**. Bueno de Mesquita, E. (2010). Regime change and revolutionary entrepreneurs. *APSR* 104(3), 446-466. ## Added in revision round 1 (2026-11-18)

All six were checked against Crossref (title, authors, venue, volume, issue, pages, year) and their abstracts read through OpenAlex. None was read in full; every source claim on them is at abstract depth.

- **morris2007**. Morris, S. and Shin, H. S. (2007). Optimal communication. *Journal of the European Economic Association* 5(2-3), 594-602. DOI 10.1162/jeea.2007.5.2-3.594. Supports: communication to a group trades precision against common understanding (abstract). Used to place Theorem 1(c) as the regime-change form of that trade-off.
- **myatt2012**. Myatt, D. P. and Wallace, C. (2012). Endogenous information acquisition in coordination games. *Review of Economic Studies* 79(1), 340-374. DOI 10.1093/restud/rdr018 (Crossref dates the advance-access version 7 October 2011; the issue year 2012 is within the identity check's tolerance). Supports: signals have an accuracy and a clarity; with more complementarity, acquired information is more public (abstract).
- **thomas2014**. Thomas, K. A., DeScioli, P., Haque, O. S. and Pinker, S. (2014). The psychology of coordination and common knowledge. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* 107(4), 657-676. DOI 10.1037/a0037037. Supports: more risky coordination under common knowledge (loudspeaker) than shared (messenger) or private knowledge (abstract). Basis of the acknowledgment cell.
- **defreitas2019**. De Freitas, J., Thomas, K., DeScioli, P. and Pinker, S. (2019). Common knowledge, coordination, and strategic mentalizing in human social life. *PNAS* 116(28), 13751-13758. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1905518116. Supports: people are highly sensitive to the distinction between common knowledge and private or shared knowledge (abstract).
- **shadmehr2011**. Shadmehr, M. and Bernhardt, D. (2011). Collective action with uncertain payoffs. *APSR* 105(4), 829-851. DOI 10.1017/S0003055411000359. Supports: public signals from other citizens can reduce the likelihood of revolt (abstract, item c).
- **levy2015**. Levy, G. and Razin, R. (2015). Correlation neglect, voting behavior, and information aggregation. *AER* 105(4), 1634-1645. DOI 10.1257/aer.20140134. Supports: correlation neglect can improve political outcomes because extreme beliefs make some voters vote on information (abstract).

The earlier decision to drop De Freitas et al. from the seed register was reversed after the referee's finding F9: the paper's experiment manipulates common knowledge of receipt, which is what that programme studies.
